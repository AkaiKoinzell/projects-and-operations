Initiation & The Project Charter

The Project's "Birth Certificate"

Before spending a single euro, the Charter formally authorizes the project. (Click any pillar to inspect)

• Formal Authority: Gives the PM power to use company resources.
• High-Level Scope: Defines the primary boundaries of the endeavor.
• Major Milestones: Outlines key dates for critical deliverables.
• Initial Budget: Provides the first financial framework for the work.

Building the Schedule

Decomposing & Sequencing

"How do we eat an elephant? One bite at a time."

  • • WBS (Work Breakdown Structure): Decomposing the total scope into manageable, bite-sized work packages (The 100% Rule).
  • • Logical Relationships: Sequencing tasks (e.g., Finish-to-Start [FS], Start-to-Start [SS], Finish-to-Finish [FF]).
  • • The Critical Path: The longest sequence of dependent tasks dictating the shortest possible project time.

Waterfall Approach

Predictive & Linear

Predictive and linear. Ideal for projects where requirements are fixed and clear up front, like building a skyscraper.

Requirements➔Design➔Build➔Test➔Big Launch

Agile Approach

Iterative & Flexible

Iterative and flexible. Embraces change through short "sprints," ideal for software where requirements evolve.

Sprint 1 (MVP) 🔄➔Feedback➔Sprint 2 🔄➔Continuous Value

1. Domains & Curve

Projects build the muscle (unique change); Operations are the heartbeat (repetitive status quo) after the Change Curve dip.

2. Governance

Authorize via the Sponsor's Charter, assign one Accountable (RACI) owner, and lock the 3 PM Plan Baselines.

3. Schedule & CPM

Decompose via WBS, map the Critical Path (Zero Float), and compress via Crashing or Fast-Tracking.

4. Waterfall vs. Agile

Use Waterfall for fixed/regulated scope; use Agile Sprints & Burndown charts for high-uncertainty products.
