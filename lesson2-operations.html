Number of units exiting the process per unit of time (e.g., per hour or per day).

The heartbeat of customer demand. Production must match this rhythm to avoid queues or waste.

3. The Reality of Capacity: Design vs. Effective

Understanding Theoretical Maximums vs. Operational Truths

👕 SHIRT FACTORY MACHINE TELEMETRY Drag sliders to test
1. Design Capacity (24/7 Ideal Max): 100 units/hr
2. Effective Capacity (After breaks/maint.): 82 units/hr
3. Actual Output Achieved Today: 74 units/hr
Utilization (Actual ÷ Design)
74.0%
Efficiency (Actual ÷ Effective)
90.2%

Design Capacity

The theoretical maximum output of a system or machine in a given period under ideal conditions (e.g., 100 shirts/hour if the machine never stops).

Effective Capacity

The capacity a firm expects to achieve given its current operating constraints (lunch breaks, maintenance, setup changeovers, power cuts — e.g., 82 shirts/hour).

Utilization = Actual Output / Design Capacity
Syllabus Companion Formula: Efficiency = Actual Output / Effective Capacity
Syllabus Deep-Dive • Pre-Reading Concept

Productivity vs. Efficiency vs. Utilization

Why do students confuse these 3 metrics? Let's compare two Baristas in Rome!

1. Productivity = Outputs ÷ Inputs

Value per Resource

Plain English: How much output do we get per euro of labor, energy, or raw material?
Example: Barista Marco makes 120 cappuccinos in an 8-hour shift = 15 coffees / labor hour.

2. Efficiency = Actual Output ÷ Effective Capacity

Doing Things Right

Plain English: Given realistic shift conditions (after cleaning & breaks), how well did we perform without waste?
Example: Realistic target is 130 coffees; Marco made 120 without spilling milk = 92.3% Efficiency.

3. Utilization = Actual Output ÷ Design Capacity

Machine Busyness

Plain English: How much of the espresso machine's theoretical 24/7 maximum capacity did we use?
Warning: 100% Utilization is dangerous! (Zero buffer for sudden tourist rushes or maintenance!).

4. Little's Law

The Fundamental Relationship of Process Flow — Click any corner of the triangle on the left to rearrange the formula!

THE 3 WAYS TO USE LITTLE'S LAW Click what a question asks you to find:
► FLOW PROCESS PIPELINE (FROM ENTRY TO EXIT)
1. CHECK-IN
Arrival (R)
2. WAITING
Queue (I)
3. CONSULT
Service
4. EXIT
Time (T)
🔢 Mode 1 — Finding Inventory (I = R × T):
When you know the arrival rate (R) and how long each unit stays inside (T), multiply them!
Pizzeria Example: 30 pizzas/hr (R) × 0.5 hours (T) = 15 pizzas inside (I).
I = R × T
Inventory (WIP) = Flow Rate × Flow Time

Inventory = Flow Rate × Flow Time

Inventory (I): The number of units currently inside the process (WIP).

Flow Rate (R): The rate at which units arrive and exit the system (Throughput).

Flow Time (T): The average time a unit spends in the system from start to finish.

⚠️ Golden Exam Rule: Always make sure R and T use the SAME time unit (both in Hours, or both in Days, or both in Minutes)!

5. Activity: Little's Law at Work

Testing the Fundamental Relationship in a Real-World Scenario — Ask the class first, then click to reveal!

FINTECH UNDERWRITING DESK SIMULATOR
Flow Rate (R — Applications / Day): 20 / day
Flow Time (T — Days to Process): 5 days
Desk WIP Inventory (I = R × T): ? (Click Reveal)
💡 Follow-Up Question for Class: Once we know I, how can the startup cut processing time (T) from 5 days down to 2 days without hiring more underwriters? Reveal ▾
✅ The Math (T = I ÷ R): Since hiring is frozen, Throughput (R = 20 apps/day) is fixed. To cut Wait Time (T) from 5 days to 2 days, we must cap Inventory (I) = 20 × 2 = 40 applications (down from 100)!
🛠️ How to do it in reality (Kanban WIP Limit): Stop dumping all 100 files onto underwriters' desks at once (which causes multitasking & context-switching waste). Cap active desk WIP at 40 files—only pull a new file in when one is finished ("Stop starting, start finishing!").
SCENARIO

A startup processes loan applications. They receive 20 applications per day (Flow Rate). On average, an application takes 5 days to process (Flow Time).

Question: How many applications are currently sitting on the team's desks (Inventory/WIP)?

👆 Click to Reveal Step-by-Step Calculation
✅ STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION:
1. Identify Given Metrics: Flow Rate (R) = 20 apps/day | Flow Time (T) = 5 days.
2. Check Time Units: Both use days! No unit conversion needed.
3. Apply Little's Law: I = R × T = 20 × 5 = 100 Applications in WIP Inventory!

1. Calculate the Hospital's Flow Rate. Reveal Step 1 ▾

✅ Customer Demand Rate: 40 patients ÷ 8 hours = 5 patients/hour (Takt Time = 480m ÷ 40 = 12 mins/patient).

• Using Doctor Capacity (R = 6 patients/hr): Wait Time T = 15 ÷ 6 = 2.5 hours (150 minutes)!
• (Using Arrival Rate R = 5 patients/hr: Wait Time T = 15 ÷ 5 = 3.0 hours / 180 minutes).
3. Where is the waste? (Preview of Section 7: Lean & Muda!) Reveal Step 3 ▾
✅ Waiting Waste (in Lean we call this "Muda"): Even though 3 doctors have enough capacity (48 patients) to beat expected demand (40 patients), 15 patients are stuck waiting 2.5+ hours because patients arrive in unscheduled Saturday-night waves, triage intake is slow, or doctors wait on lab tests!

6. Bottlenecks & Sensitivity Analysis

Optimizing Throughput by Identifying the Limiting Factor — Ask the class first, then click to reveal!

CONCEPT

A process is only as fast as its slowest step. That is the Bottleneck. It dictates your total system revenue.

Class Question: Look at the 3 steps below. Which step is the Bottleneck, and what is the maximum hourly output of this factory?

Step A
50 u/h
➔
Step B
20 u/h
➔
Step C
60 u/h
🎯 Answer Revealed: Step B (20 u/h) is the Bottleneck! Even though Step A can do 50 u/h and Step C can do 60 u/h, total system output is capped at exactly 20 units/hour.
System Throughput = Min(Capacity of All Steps)
SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS (THE €5,000 QUESTION)

Assume each finished unit sells for €100. Ask the class what happens in each scenario, then click to reveal:

Scenario 1: Spend €5k to upgrade Step C to 80 u/h Click to Reveal ▾
❌ Extra Revenue? ZERO (€0/hr)! Because Step B still only feeds 20 units/hr into Step C, upgrading Step C from 60 to 80 u/h produces zero extra units!
Scenario 2: Spend €5k to upgrade Step B to 25 u/h Click to Reveal ▾
✅ Revenue jumps to €2,500/hr (+€500/hr)! Upgrading the Bottleneck by +5 u/h immediately increases total plant output from 20 to 25 u/h. Pays for itself in 10 hours!
💡 Goldratt's Law: "An hour saved at a non-bottleneck is a mirage." Always run Sensitivity Analysis on the Bottleneck first!

7. The Story of Lean & Eliminating Muda

Optimizing Value by Removing Non-Value-Added Activities (Taiichi Ohno & Toyota Production System)

THE TOYOTA LEGACY

Post-WWII, Toyota lacked the cash of Ford or GM. They couldn't afford warehouses of inventory.

They invented Lean: a relentless obsession with eliminating Muda (waste).

Manager's Goal: Map the Value Stream and cut anything the customer won't pay for.

The Customer Acid Test: "If the customer saw us doing this step, would they happily pay extra for it?" If NO ➔ It is Muda!
DEFINITION & "TIMWOODS" 8 WASTES EXPLORER Click any letter to see real examples

Muda is waste: waiting for emails, over-production, or extra steps to find a tool.

🚚 T — Transportation Muda: Moving products, materials, or files unnecessarily between distant rooms (e.g., slicing bread 50 meters away from the meat fridge in Airline Catering).

8. The Measurement Illusion

Understanding Lagging vs. Leading Indicators — Switch industries on the right to see real examples!

LAGGING INDICATORS

The Rearview Mirror

Financial Metrics: Net Profit and EBITDA.

They tell you what already happened. You cannot directly command or change them on the shop floor today.

🥖 Bakery Lagging Outcome: Quarterly Net Profit Margin (+2% Goal) & Monthly Flour COGS.
Role in Executive P&L: The Real Outcome (Result of Inputs)
LEADING INDICATORS

The Windshield

Operational Metrics: Cycle Time and Defect Rate.

They tell you where the car is going before you crash. Operators can directly control and adjust these every hour!

🥖 Bakery Leading Input: Dough-cutter blade calibration every 2 hrs & loaf weight variance (< 2g overweight).
Formula Connection: Controllable Input (Leading) ➔ Real Outcome (Lagging)
